{# La Quiniela live board — NOT a playlist slide. Included once, as live DOM, inside #slideshow-stage (slideshow.html for /slides, board.html for /board), after the two slide layers and before the overlays. Hidden by default; static/js/quiniela_board.js subscribes to /api/quiniela/stream and crossfades this layer in while the model's race_state is one of the page's board states (data-board-states, which the server writes), holding the playlist in place through window.ddmSlideshow. Styles: static/css/quiniela_board.css. Design: tools/board_reference.html. Everything the JS touches is addressed by id or data-horse. The rows are not rendered here: the two columns start empty and the JS fills them from the model's field (every horse with in_field true, in numeric order, the first ten left and the rest right; a short field leaves the trailing slots empty), cloning #qb-row-tpl for each horse. So a scratched horse never has a row and an also-eligible that draws in (21-24) gets one where its number sorts. The stage (.qb-stage) holds two views, and the board's data-view says which one shows: the two columns ("rows"), or the results screen ("results") once the race is over and the model carries La Quiniela's payout: one row per prize paid, the horse whose cup is drawn from (two or three in a dead heat) with its cloth and name, the bets its cup held and its share, and the finisher skipped for no bets. The JS writes all of it. data-look is the look the page was asked for (server.board_look(): ?look=, else config.QUINIELA_LOOK): "impact", "dots" or "numbers". Both looks are this one layout; data-tote marks the tote's fields, the ones the tote look draws in its dot-matrix face ("num": a figure, "name": a horse's name; the crawl's text is marked by the JS). Cloths, the banner, the toast and every label stay as they are. #}
